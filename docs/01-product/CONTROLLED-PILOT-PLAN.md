# NoorPath Controlled Pilot Plan

Status: Future readiness artifact  
Owner: Product  
Activation condition: NoorPath MVP is functionally ready for a real operator-backed pilot  
Purpose: Validate customer trust, operator fulfilment, payment handling, refund readiness, and end-to-end booking operations before wider launch.

## 1. Pilot objective

Run one controlled, operator-backed Umrah booking pilot to determine whether NoorPath can safely convert real customer intent into a fulfilled journey.

This is not a public launch.

The pilot must answer:

- Do prospective pilgrims understand and trust the package?
- Does the booking flow reduce friction compared with agent-led booking?
- Do instalments increase willingness to book?
- Can one operator reliably fulfil the complete workflow?
- Can payments, reconciliation, cancellations, refunds, documents, visas, rooms, flights, and support be handled without critical failure?
- Is NoorPath ready to scale beyond a small supervised batch?

## 2. Pilot scope

Use only:

- one verified Umrah operator;
- one departure city;
- one real package;
- one fixed departure batch;
- approximately 10–15 qualified prospective customers;
- a small seat allocation;
- tightly supervised operations;
- one clearly defined payment and refund model.

Do not run multiple operators, cities, or packages in the first pilot.

## 3. Mandatory pre-pilot gates

The pilot must not accept meaningful customer funds until all gates below are complete.

### Operator verification

Verify and retain evidence of:

- legal business registration;
- tax and banking details;
- responsible directors or proprietors;
- relevant travel and Umrah operating permissions;
- Saudi-side company or approved partner relationship;
- prior pilgrim references;
- hotel, flight, transport, visa, and ground-handling process;
- cancellation and refund policy;
- support ownership and escalation contacts.

### Pilot agreement

A signed agreement must define:

- merchant of record;
- party receiving customer money;
- NoorPath commission or service fee;
- settlement timing;
- seat-inventory ownership;
- responsibility for visa, flight, hotel, transfer, meals, and ziyarah fulfilment;
- cancellation authority;
- source of refund funds;
- refund timeline;
- customer-support responsibilities;
- operator service levels;
- incident escalation;
- data-protection responsibilities;
- treatment of operator failure or trip cancellation.

### Legal and policy readiness

Complete review of:

- customer terms;
- privacy notice;
- passport and sensitive-document processing;
- payment and settlement flow;
- cancellation rules;
- refund rules;
- liability and disclaimers;
- customer grievance process;
- operator obligations.

### Operational readiness

Confirm:

- named pilot owner;
- named operator contact;
- support hours;
- escalation matrix;
- reconciliation process;
- incident log;
- manual fallback procedures;
- refund execution process;
- backup and recovery for critical records.

## 4. Pilot package requirements

The pilot package must be real and fully specified.

It must clearly show:

- departure city;
- departure and return dates;
- airline or flight status;
- baggage allowance;
- Makkah and Madinah hotels;
- approximate hotel distance;
- room occupancy options;
- meals;
- ground transfers;
- ziyarah inclusions;
- visa inclusion and process;
- travel kit and Umrah kit;
- total price;
- deposit or first instalment;
- remaining instalment schedule;
- payment deadlines;
- inclusions;
- exclusions;
- cancellation charges;
- refund timeline;
- operator identity;
- support contact.

No ambiguous “subject to availability” language may hide a material package commitment.

## 5. Customer recruitment

Recruit 10–15 qualified prospective customers from the intended market.

Prefer:

- first-time pilgrims;
- family organisers;
- customers requiring instalments;
- customers from Tier-2 or Tier-3 cities;
- elderly travellers represented by a family member;
- customers who normally use a local agent;
- previous Umrah travellers able to compare experiences.

Avoid relying mainly on friends, software professionals, or people with no genuine travel intent.

Record recruitment source and qualification reason.

## 6. Customer test journey

Each participant should attempt the journey with minimal guidance:

1. Discover the package.
2. Understand the operator and package credibility.
3. Review itinerary and inclusions.
4. Compare room occupancy.
5. Add travellers or family members.
6. Understand the total amount and instalments.
7. Review cancellation and refund terms.
8. Upload or simulate passport documents.
9. Understand document-review and visa stages.
10. Ask a support question.
11. Reach the payment or refundable-reservation decision.
12. Explain the package and refund terms back in their own words.

Observers must record where customers hesitate, misunderstand, abandon, or ask for human reassurance.

## 7. Payment approach

Use the lowest-risk payment method that still produces valid evidence.

Preferred sequence:

1. payment simulation or intent capture;
2. small refundable reservation deposit;
3. tightly controlled real booking payment only after legal and operational approval.

Before taking real funds, define:

- merchant of record;
- gateway account owner;
- settlement destination;
- operator payout process;
- commission deduction;
- payment reconciliation;
- failed-payment handling;
- chargeback handling;
- partial cancellation;
- refund initiation and tracking;
- accounting evidence.

Do not manually collect and redistribute substantial funds without an approved legal, accounting, and payment structure.

## 8. Operator workflow observation

Observe the operator completing the actual workflow.

Track:

- package creation and publication;
- seat inventory updates;
- booking review;
- traveller verification;
- payment reconciliation;
- document review;
- correction requests;
- visa-case processing;
- room allocation;
- flight confirmation;
- customer communication;
- cancellation calculation;
- refund processing;
- support response;
- incident resolution.

Manual supervision is acceptable during the pilot. Hidden manual work must be recorded so it is not mistaken for scalable automation.

## 9. Customer interview guide

Immediately after the journey, ask:

- What made you trust or distrust the package?
- What information was missing?
- At what point would you normally call an agent?
- What would stop you from paying today?
- Did instalments make the trip more affordable?
- Who else in your family must approve the booking?
- Which step felt most risky?
- Who should hold your money: NoorPath or the operator?
- What refund timeframe would you accept?
- Did the room-occupancy explanation make sense?
- Did you understand when flights and visas become confirmed?
- Would you use NoorPath for a real booking?
- Would you recommend it to a relative?

Do not rely on “Do you like the app?” as validation.

Collect:

### Customer metrics

- qualified prospects;
- onboarding completion;
- package-detail completion;
- room-selection comprehension;
- family-member addition completion;
- instalment comprehension;
- refund-term comprehension;
- document-flow completion;
- support requests;
- payment intent;
- deposit conversion;
- abandonment point;
- trust rating;
- recommendation intent.

### Operator metrics

- inventory accuracy;
- response time;
- document-review time;
- correction turnaround;
- visa-status accuracy;
- reconciliation accuracy;
- refund turnaround;
- support SLA compliance;
- manual interventions;
- operational exceptions.

### Safety metrics

- payment discrepancies;
- privacy incidents;
- document-access violations;
- incorrect traveller linkage;
- unauthorised status changes;
- refund disputes;
- unresolved customer complaints;
- critical fulfilment failures.

## 11. Initial pilot thresholds

These are internal test thresholds, not industry benchmarks.

| Measure | Initial threshold |
|---|---:|
| Qualified prospects | 15 or more |
| Complete core booking journey | 80% or more |
| Correctly understand total price and instalments | 90% or more |
| Correctly understand cancellation and refund terms | 90% or more |
| Willing to place a deposit | 30–40% or more |
| Payment reconciliation discrepancies | 0 |
| Passport-data security incidents | 0 |
| Critical trust complaints | 0 |
| Operator critical fulfilment failures | 0 |
| Operator workflow completed within agreed controls | 100% |

## 12. Decision rules

### Proceed

Proceed to a broader pilot when:

- customers understand and trust the package;
- real payment intent exists;
- the operator fulfils reliably;
- reconciliation is accurate;
- refunds and support are controllable;
- no critical privacy, security, or trust issue remains.

### Revise

Revise and repeat the pilot when:

- customers want the product but specific trust, pricing, wording, support, or usability issues block conversion;
- the operator can fulfil but workflows require redesign;
- manual intervention is high but reducible.

### Hold

Hold launch when:

- merchant-of-record responsibility is unclear;
- operator licensing or Saudi-side relationships are unverified;
- refund funding or authority is unclear;
- legal or privacy review is incomplete;
- reconciliation cannot be proven.

### Stop

Stop the pilot when:

- customer funds or sensitive documents cannot be protected;
- the operator cannot fulfil commitments;
- customers materially misunderstand price or refund obligations;
- serious trust failures occur;
- operational risk cannot be contained.

## 13. Pilot outputs

At completion, produce:

- pilot summary;
- evidence register;
- customer findings;
- operator findings;
- conversion funnel;
- reconciliation report;
- incident report;
- privacy and security findings;
- manual-work inventory;
- pass/revise/hold/stop decision;
- approved changes for the next numbered slice or release.

## 14. Activation checklist

Do not activate this plan until:

- the MVP supports the full core journey;
- the Product Owner declares pilot readiness;
- the operator is selected and verified;
- legal, payment, privacy, and refund gates are complete;
- one real package is ready;
- support and incident owners are named;
- the pilot cohort is recruited;
- production monitoring and backups are verified.

This document is intentionally stored before launch so NoorPath can be evaluated against a pre-agreed evidence standard rather than subjective impressions after release.
